🚁 QyranEye · prototype

Find the person who fell — before it is too late.

QyranEye watches drone video and raises an alarm when a person falls and stays down. A rescuer then checks that spot first, instead of watching hours of footage.

How it works

Recorded drone video in, alarms out. A person always makes the final decision.

  1. Find people in every frame (YOLO26 neural network) and follow each one, correcting for the drone's own movement.
  2. Read the body posture: standing, in between, or lying — from the shape of the person and, for larger people, from body keypoints (shoulders, hips, ankles).
  3. Fall or lie down? How fast the shoulders move relative to the feet: fast = fall, slow = lying down on purpose.
  4. Confirm: the person must really be seen lying and not moving for 4 seconds. Then: alarm. Lying still 10 s without a fall: CHECK.

Try it with your own video

Upload a drone video up to 30 s and choose the camera view. It runs on a free cloud server: the first visit can take ~30 s to wake up, and one video takes about 2–5 minutes.

Not loading? Open the demo directly: zhangir954--qyraneye-ui.us-east.modal.run. Uploaded videos are processed in a temporary folder and deleted; nothing is kept.

Where we are — honestly

  • Tested on public drone datasets (Okutama-Action, NOMAD, MOD20): no false alarms on the clips we checked, but these contain no real falls — only one real fall video so far.
  • Next: our own drone filming (FIMI X8 SE, 5–20 m) with 36 staged falls and 72 normal actions, scored once on people the system was never tuned on.
  • Hard cases today: very small people (< 80 px), straight-down camera, people hidden in grass, and lying down quickly on purpose (looks like a fall — the operator decides).
